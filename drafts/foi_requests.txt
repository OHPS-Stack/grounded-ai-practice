REQUEST 1 of 2
AUTHORITY: Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport
   (NOT DSIT - abolished 21 July 2026. If DCMS redirects, follow the
   redirect; do not send to two departments in parallel.)
SUMMARY / SUBJECT LINE:
   AI Skills Boost course completion figure (January 2026)
================================================================

--- START OF REQUEST 1 ---

This is a request under the Freedom of Information Act 2000. It concerns a figure published by the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology before its functions were redistributed on 21 July 2026; responsibility for digital inclusion and skills moved to DCMS. If another department now holds any part of what I ask for, please transfer that part or tell me who to approach.

In January 2026 the government reported that 1,001,147 AI training courses had been completed under the AI Skills Boost programme. The figure appears in "AI Skills Boost: explainer" (GOV.UK, 28 January 2026), which describes it as "course completion data shared with DSIT by industry partners in January 2026". Please provide:

1. The definition of a "completed" course used for that figure, and any guidance or reporting template issued to the partner companies on what to count.

2. How many of the 1,001,147 completions were by the partner companies' own employees, and how many were by external learners (their customers, clients and platform users).

3. Whether the figure is limited to learners in the UK, and how learner location was determined.

4. How many individual people completed those courses, if this is held. One person completing several courses counts several times in a course total; I am asking how many people the total represents.

5. How many of the counted courses were checked against Skills England's "AI foundation skills for work" benchmark, and how many met it.

6. Any recorded method for measuring progress towards the target of 10 million workers upskilled by 2030 using these course completions, for example how completions are related to individual workers.

If answering everything would exceed the cost limit, please tell me which parts can be answered within it rather than refusing the request in full.

--- END OF REQUEST 1 ---

REQUEST 2 of 2
AUTHORITY: UK Research and Innovation
   (NOT "Innovate UK" - UKRI handles its FOIs, and the references
   cited are UKRI's own.)
SUMMARY / SUBJECT LINE:
   BridgeAI delivery figures and AI Skills Hub reporting (UKRI-3797)
================================================================

--- START OF REQUEST 2 ---

This is a request under the Freedom of Information Act 2000, about the BridgeAI programme and the AI Skills Hub.

On BridgeAI, as reported in "Bridging the AI divide: Year three in review" (March 2026):

1. For the reported figure of 1,700+ AI skills courses completed: the definition of "completed" used, whether the unit counted is a module or a full course, and, if held, how many individual people the figure represents.

2. How the £74.6 million reported as allocated by the end of 2025 breaks down, by part of the programme or by delivery partner, in whatever form you hold it.

3. Any evaluation of BridgeAI, whether internal or commissioned, or the timetable for one if it is planned but not yet complete.

On the AI Skills Hub, which PwC runs under contract UKRI-3797 (specification disclosed under FOI2026/00071):

4. The Hub's performance figures: how many people have used it, and how many individuals and organisations have taken training as a result of it, for each quarter since it launched. PwC is required to report these figures quarterly under the contract (Annex A of the specification), in anonymised form. I am asking for the numbers themselves, not for copies of PwC's quarterly reports, which were withheld under FOI2026/00204.

5. The second white paper on skills and training gaps. The first was disclosed under FOI2026/00458 and states at page 79 that a second was due within six months. Please also provide any KPI breakdown covering periods after the one disclosed with it.

6. Whether the Hub's contract was extended when its initial period ended in March 2026 (the contract provided for a 12-month extension decided at that point), and the service's current contractual status.

If answering everything would exceed the cost limit, items 4 and 5 matter most, and I would rather have those than a refusal of the whole request. Where an answer is a table of numbers, a spreadsheet format such as CSV or Excel is preferred to a PDF.

--- END OF REQUEST 2 ---
