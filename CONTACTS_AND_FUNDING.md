## Purpose

Two connected things:

1. **People and bodies worth approaching for comment**, to move the
   project's published output from a personal research project toward
   something that meets ordinary standards for a credible research
   document.
2. **Realistic routes to funding or official support**, tracked as they
   surface.

These are separate goals with different timings, and the register keeps
them separate deliberately (see "Sequencing" below).

## Sequencing — read before acting on anything here

The order these are done in materially affects whether they work.

1. **Freedom of Information requests first.** Free, burn no
   relationships, and produce citable primary evidence the project
   currently lacks. The AI Skills Hub's missing usage and completion
   figures (`RESEARCH_LOG.md` Entry 044) are the obvious first target.
2. **Academic comment second.** Low risk, high credibility gain.
   Researchers commenting on their own published work is routine practice.
   Several named below have independently published findings close to
   this project's own.
3. **Right of reply third — and before publication, never after.**
   Any organisation the project's output criticises should be offered the
   chance to respond before that output is published. This is standard
   practice, materially strengthens the document, and avoids the
   appearance of an ambush. "Approached for comment; declined" is a
   legitimate and useful line. Publishing first and asking afterwards is
   not.
4. **Funding approaches last.** These are strongest when there is a
   built, tested pilot and a credible published document behind them.
   Approaching a funder early, with work still in draft, spends a first
   impression cheaply.

## A tension to manage deliberately

The project intends to publish work critical of a government-funded
programme, and separately hopes for official support or funding from
bodies adjacent to that programme.

These are not incompatible — "here is the gap, here is a fix" is a
legitimate position — but they need handling as two distinct
conversations. Combining a critique and a funding request in a single
approach risks reading as leverage rather than research.

Open question for the creator, not yet decided: is the published document
a **credential** that earns a later conversation, or an **opening move**
in one? These imply different documents.

## Funding and support routes

First research pass completed 2026-07-28. Not exhaustive — a starting map,
not a shortlist. **All eligibility below is as stated on the funders' own
pages at that date and must be re-checked before any application**;
schemes open, close and change criteria frequently.

### The two gates that shape everything else

Before individual schemes, two structural constraints determine which
routes are even open:

**Gate 1 — most business innovation funding requires a registered UK
entity.** Innovate UK's stated eligibility covers limited companies,
partnerships, **sole traders**, academic institutions, charities, public
sector organisations, **Community Interest Companies (CICs)** and RTOs.
An unregistered personal project is not eligible. Two observations:
- The bar is lower than it first appears. Sole-trader registration is
  administratively light, so this is not a company-formation requirement.
- A CIC may be the better structural fit for a project whose stated aim
  is public benefit rather than commercial return, and is explicitly
  eligible. Worth proper advice before choosing — this is a decision with
  tax, liability and governance consequences well outside what this
  register should attempt to advise on.

**Gate 2 — most research fellowship funding requires an academic post.**
The British Academy's Innovation Fellowships (Route B, Policy-led) state
plainly: *"Independent researchers who do not hold a post at a HEI or IRO
are not eligible to apply for this scheme."* This matters more than it
looks, because **this is the scheme that funded the PRIMES research this
project relies on** — Dr Nisreen Ameen's work was a British Academy
Policy-Led Innovation Fellowship. The route that produced the project's
most-used framework is closed to the project as currently constituted.
The realistic implication is that this class of funding is reachable
**only in partnership with an eligible academic**, which connects
directly to the academic contacts listed above.

### Precedent worth noting

The **AI Upskilling Fund** was a £7.4m pilot (£6.4m in grants) subsidising
AI skills training for SMEs in the Professional Business Services sector.
It closed to applications in August 2024. Two reasons to keep it on file:
it shows government has funded AI skills provision through channels
separate from the AI Skills Hub, and any published evaluation of it would
be directly relevant comparative evidence for the UK-climate report.

### Assessment of current readiness

Recorded plainly rather than optimistically: **the project is not yet at
a stage where most of these routes would be competitive.** The pilot unit
is decided but not built; no learner testing has happened; the flagship
document is paused mid-draft. Nearly every scheme above expects either a
registered entity, an academic partner, or demonstrable delivery.

This is an argument for sequencing, not discouragement. The Ufi
"VocTech Together" tier (small grant plus mentoring) is the most
plausible early-stage entry point of those found, because it is designed
for planning and implementation support rather than scaled delivery, and
carries no conflict with the project's critical position.
